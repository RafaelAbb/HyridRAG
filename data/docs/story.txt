Once every fifty years, the Tide-Weaver’s clockwork lighthouse required a new heart.

Silas, a boy with brass-rimmed spectacles and permanently ink-stained fingertips, was this generation’s chosen apprentice. He spent his days in the highest tower, polishing gears that hummed with the trapped energy of ocean storms. The lighthouse didn’t warn ships away from rocks; it wove the very currents that kept the floating island of Oakhaven from drifting into the Endless Void.

Today was the Day of Alignment, and the old Weaver had vanished, leaving only a note and a glowing sapphire core.

"The sea demands balance, Silas. Do not let the rhythm break."

Silas swallowed the lump in his throat and looked out at the churning, violet waves below. The lighthouse began to shudder, its massive brass pendulum losing its steady cadence. Tick... tick... pause.

He scrambled up the iron scaffolding, the heavy sapphire core burning cold against his chest. Dust rained down as the outer walls groaned under the sudden, violent shift in the wind. Oakhaven tilted three degrees to the west, sending pots and pans clattering through the town square below. Silas reached the central housing, where the old core was fading into a dull, ash gray.

With trembling hands, he unscrewed the primary brass housing, the heat scorching his palms. The moment the dead core slipped out, the lighthouse went dead silent, and the island plunged into a terrifying freefall.

Silas didn't panic; he closed his eyes and listened to the phantom rhythm he had studied for years. He slammed the sapphire core into the empty socket and twisted it until the gears bit.

A brilliant blue light erupted from the tower, slicing through the storm clouds. The gears ground to a halt, then violently surged forward with a triumphant, resonant CHUNG.

The island stabilized with a gentle jolt, anchoring itself safely back into the sky-currents. Silas collapsed against the railing, his face smudged with soot but a brilliant grin breaking through.

He was no longer just an apprentice. He was the Weaver now, and the horizon was finally clear.
