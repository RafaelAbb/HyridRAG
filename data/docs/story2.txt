Six months had passed since Silas became the Tide-Weaver, and the sky-currents around Oakhaven had never been smoother. But compliance from the sea was a rare luxury, and the horizon was about to change.

An urgent, erratic clicking echoed from the lighthouse’s lowest chamber—the deep-sea telegraph was alive for the first time in a decade.

Silas rushed down the spiral stairs, his brass-rimmed spectacles slipping down his nose. The machine was punching out a frantic code onto a strip of copper tape. When Silas deciphered the jagged punctures, his blood ran cold:

“Sinking. Current collapsed. Need a Weaver.”

The distress signal came from the Sunken Archives, a neighboring floating library that had lost its buoyancy and plunged into the cloud-oceans below. Without hesitation, Silas packed his leather satchel with a portable resonance tuner and leaped onto the lighthouse’s emergency skiff, the Zephyr.

He dove into the thick, violet mist of the Endless Void, relying entirely on his instruments to guide him through the blind whiteout.

The pressure mounted, making the wood of the skiff groan as he descended into the dark. Finally, the Zephyr’s spotlights caught a magnificent, decaying structure of marble and iron, trapped inside a massive, dying whirlpool. The Archives were spinning downward into the crushing depths.

Silas moored his skiff to a crumbling balustrade and bolted inside, navigating through floating, water-logged scrolls. In the subterranean engine room, he found the culprit: a massive, rusted sky-anchor had jammed the main ballast gears, choking the flow of the current.

The structure lurched violently, dropping another hundred feet into the darkness.

Silas pulled out his resonance tuner, striking it against his boot until it hummed with a high-pitched, vibrant ring. He pressed the vibrating metal directly against the jammed anchor, searching for its structural weakness. There.

With a heavy wrench, he struck the exact point of highest tension.

The iron anchor shattered like glass. Instantly, the pent-up sky-currents roared back to life, filling the buoyancy chambers with brilliant blue energy. The Archives groaned, paused, and then rocketed upward, breaking through the dark clouds and bursting back into the warm sunlight.

Silas stood on the deck of the rising library, catching his breath as the librarians cheered. Another corner of the world had been mapped, saved, and woven back into place.
